08 FAQ
Questions, answered
More in the glossary and guides .
What is TrustGraph?
For enterprises deploying AI agents where decisions carry real risk, TrustGraph is the open-source ontology runtime that turns all your data, including documents, structured data and images, into ontology-grounded knowledge, and governs what every agent and user can see from it. Unlike platform ontologies from Palantir, Microsoft, Databricks and Snowflake, TrustGraph is built on W3C standards (RDF, OWL, SHACL, PROV-O) and Apache 2.0, so your ontology, your knowledge and your audit trail stay yours.
How is this different from Palantir's Ontology?
Palantir's Ontology is proprietary and lives inside Foundry. TrustGraph uses W3C standards, is Apache 2.0, and runs anywhere; you can export your ontology and knowledge as Turtle.
Do I need an existing ontology?
No. Start with a handful of classes for one question, or begin with schema-free GraphRAG and add an ontology later.
What is a context graph?
A context graph connects business entities, relationships and evidence so AI agents retrieve knowledge with its full meaning and its source. TrustGraph builds context graphs, the technique Thoughtworks' Technology Radar recommends assessing for agentic AI.
How is this different from AI guardrails?
Guardrails filter what a model says after it has seen the data. TrustGraph's SHACL-AF policies filter what an agent or user can see in the first place: every graph query is evaluated node by node and each result is Allowed, Filtered or a Violation, with the evaluation recorded.
Which standards does TrustGraph use?
W3C RDF (with RDF-star), OWL, SHACL-AF, PROV-O and SPARQL 1.1, plus MCP for agent access. Your ontologies, policies and provenance stay portable with no lock-in.
Is TrustGraph open source?
Yes, the core is Apache 2.0. Enterprise add-ons cover knowledge-based access controls and IAM, an attestation engine and temporal knowledge management, with engineering support for production deployments.
