history
the social recession...
& how we got there
1900-1949
The early 20th century was highly social, with surging church and labor union membership, record-high marriage and birth rates post WWII,
and thriving associations like book clubs and volunteer groups. The New Deal established third spaces like libraries, theaters, and playgrounds.
1950-1999
"Once Americans got out of the car, they planted themselves in front of the television. From 1965 to 1995, the typical adult gained six hours a week in leisure time."
Derek Thompson, The Anti-Social Century, The Atlantic
The rise of highways, suburbs, automobiles, and television led to a sharp decline in third places; the backbone of social fabric. TV dominated leisure, reducing participation in volunteering, social clubs, dinner parties, PTAs, religious institutions, libraries, and sports leagues.
2000-2019
“By this account, screens occupy more than 30 percent of waking life."
The internet, smartphones, and social media reshaped leisure, heavily impacting younger generations. Teens now spend 270-380 minutes daily on screens.
2020-2022
The COVID-19 pandemic intensified social isolation and remote work, diminishing time at workplaces & universities (the last of third places).
This shift further eroded opportunities for spontaneous social interaction, deepening feelings of loneliness and disconnection across communities.
2023-2025
“You can set them (AIs) up to never criticize you, never cheat on you, never have a bad day and insult you, and to always be interested in you.”
AI companions and virtual worlds offer flawless, customizable relationships, potentially replacing human connections with machine-based validation. As time spent with other humans falls even more, we’ve become more politically divided & far less likely for form long lasting romantic relationships. Rising civic divide, political intolerance, & a plummeting birth rate have become the natural conclusions.
present day
We are now at a societal inflection point. As more & more of blue & white collar labor is automated, we will be faced with an abundance of leisure time. Will we spend this time retreating into a fully virtual & solitary existence?
Or on the other side of the spectrum, will we spend this time in real life, exploring our cities, deepening our relationships, and enriching our life experiences with the people we value most.

thesis.
we are deeply unsatisfied with today’s so-called social products. instead of fostering human connection, they’ve drained meaning from our lives while exploiting our time and attention. vanity metrics and identity-group affiliations have corroded our social fabric and dulled our sense of connection with the nearby stranger. infinite choice and endless information about people and places have left us paralyzed rather than connected.
instead of meeting each other, we stay home, drowning in decision fatigue, distraction, and infinite entertainment. the friction between digital life and meaningful in-person interaction has pushed us into a social recession: the death of third places, fewer close friends than ever before, rising division, and declining birth rates.
we are hell-bent on reversing this. we believe humanity faces a great filter; mass social isolation combined with hyper-realistic simulations that trap us in distraction. if left unchecked, we risk deeper division and disconnection. more information and more choice are not liberating, they are paralyzing.
hick's law tells us that the more choices we face, the harder it is to decide. the same is true for how we meet people and experience places. the more digital preconceptions we accumulate, the less likely we are to commit to showing up in real life.
our product, brand, and marketing reject this premise. we design experiences that strip away digital preconceptions, making space for authentic irl encounters. as artificial intelligence floods the virtual world with synthetic content and chatbot companions, true human connection will only become more scarce and more valuable.
there are already enough people living close by to make meaningful connections at the tap of a button. with the right technology and the right team, we can build a future where we spend the majority of our time with the people and places in our lives.
