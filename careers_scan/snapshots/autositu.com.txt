Capabilities
What AutoSitu does for you here.
All-division coverage
Roughly fourteen working divisions across sitework, structure, envelope, interiors and MEP — so a set produces a complete quantity picture rather than a few trades.
Element-level quantities
Areas, lengths, counts and volumes derived from the building and site elements themselves — walls, slabs, openings, finishes, fixtures, paving, utilities and earthwork.
Organized the way you estimate
Quantities are grouped by division and by system, with level and area breakdowns, so they drop into your estimate structure instead of fighting it.
Floor-by-floor and area breakdowns
See quantities per level, per building and per phase, which is what makes scheduling, buyout packages and progress billing straightforward.
Scope and buyout gaps
Beyond quantities: where the documents are ambiguous, where scope is undefined, and where an assumption is carrying risk into the bid.
Buyout and bid packages
Turn division quantities into trade packages so subcontractors bid the same defined scope — and so you can compare their numbers against yours.
Rerun on the next issue
When a revised set lands, run it again and compare. Quantity deltas by division are the fastest way to understand what a design change actually costs.
Auditable output
Export structured quantities with their source references for your estimating system, your owner, or the record on a change-order conversation.
How it works
From upload to a decision you can defend.
Step 01
Upload the set
Architectural, structural, civil and MEP drawings as issued. No modeling, tagging or manual markup step first.
Step 02
Elements get read
AutoSitu builds a structured understanding of the building and site — elements, assemblies, levels and areas — from the documents themselves.
Step 03
Quantities by division
Measured quantities are compiled by CSI division and system, with level and area breakdowns and the source reference for each figure.
Step 04
Estimate, package, buy out
Take the quantities into pricing, split them into trade packages, and rerun on the next issue to see exactly what changed.
