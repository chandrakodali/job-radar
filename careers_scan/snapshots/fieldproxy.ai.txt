The whole topic
Preventive maintenance and service agreements, end to end
Recurring work is several jobs at once: knowing what equipment you look after, promising visits, creating them on time, doing them properly, billing for them and renewing the promise. This page covers all of it at a summary level and links to the deeper page for each part.
The agreement is the source of truth
A service agreement holds the sites and equipment covered, the visits included, response times, what is billed and when it renews. Everything else on this page is driven from it. If you are writing your first one, the service agreement template walkthrough shows how a paper template becomes a live agreement, and the guide to managing customer service agreements covers terms, pricing and renewals in depth.
Visits that create themselves
Each agreement creates its next visits ahead of time from the cadence, so nobody keys them in from a spreadsheet. How that works for a quarterly contract is answered step by step in contracts that create the next visit .
Equipment and its history
Preventive maintenance is planned per unit as well as per site: its type, age, warranty, run-hours and every visit, part and photo. The asset management page covers the equipment register and QR codes, and equipment history tracking explains what to record on each visit and why.
Maintenance work orders and the backlog
Planned visits sit next to requests from customers and repairs from failed inspection items. If your work is mostly maintaining equipment across many sites, maintenance management software goes deeper on maintenance work orders, PM completion and backlog by site, and how this compares with a CMMS.
Routes, crews and capacity
Recurring visits land on the same schedule as reactive work and are grouped by area so a week is not overloaded. Teams that run weekly rounds can plan weekly maintenance runs from a job list .
Billing, memberships and renewals
Agreements bill up front, monthly, quarterly or per visit, with AutoPay, and care plans for homeowners renew ahead of time. Invoices, payment links and accounting sync are covered on estimates and invoicing .
What the customer sees
Customers can see their plan, their next visit, past reports and what they owe in the customer portal , so the office is not asked when the next service is due.
Agents that watch the contracts
The Scheduler agent proposes dates for the visits that are due and the Watcher agent flags the missed visit, the contract running out of included hours and the renewal coming up. Changes wait for a person to approve them.

How preventive maintenance contracts schedule themselves
A contractor has quarterly HVAC maintenance across dozens of commercial sites and an annual plan for homeowners. Here is how a year of visits is created, booked, done and billed.
The contract sets the cadence
Riverside Plaza is on a Gold agreement: quarterly HVAC PPM, a four-hour response time and included filter changes. The agreement holds the sites, the units and the terms.
Visits are created ahead
The year's visits are generated with their dates and slotted into existing capacity, assigned to the technician or crew who usually covers the site.
A visit slips, and someone is told
A Q4 visit at Harbour Point is three days overdue. A rule watching every cadence alerts the office, and the visit is rebooked with the cadence kept.
Billing follows the contract
The agreement bills monthly by AutoPay. Visits beyond what is included are invoiced separately.
Renewal is prepared
Ninety days before the renewal date, a renewal is drafted and sent, counted from the date on the contract so it fires even if nobody opens it.
No maintenance visit is silently dropped, and the contract shows billed to date, cost to serve and margin as the year goes on.

Worked example: a homeowner membership
How a residential care plan runs for a year
The worked example above is a commercial contract. This one is a homeowner on a two-visit HVAC care plan, the most common membership in residential heating and cooling. It shows how the plan is sold, scheduled, billed and renewed.
Sold at the end of a repair
A technician finishes a furnace repair for Melissa Coleman and offers the Comfort plan on the phone at checkout: a spring cooling tune-up, an autumn heating tune-up, priority booking and a member discount on repairs. She signs on the phone and her card is kept on file for AutoPay.
The plan creates two visits
Joining the plan creates the spring and autumn visits for the year with their target months. Both carry the tune-up checklist for her equipment, so the technician records readings, filter size and condition the same way every time.
Booked inside the window
When the spring window opens, the visit is proposed on a day when a technician is already working nearby. The booking message waits for the office to send it, and Melissa confirms the time from her text or portal.
The visit is done and recorded
The technician opens the visit, scans the QR label on the outdoor unit, works through the checklist and adds photos. A worn capacitor is found. Because she is a member, the repair quote shows her member price and she approves it on the spot.
Billing runs without a person
The monthly plan payment is taken by AutoPay. The capacitor repair is invoiced separately at member pricing and paid with Tap to Pay before the technician leaves.
Renewal is prepared ahead
Ninety days before the anniversary, the renewal is drafted from the plan, counted from the date on the record.
The membership, both visits, the repair and every payment sit on Melissa's record. The plan shows visits used and remaining, and the care plans screen shows members, renewals due and visits due across every customer.
