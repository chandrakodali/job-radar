Program
Our program is an evidence-based, physician-led program that teaches patients why lifestyle changes impact insulin resistance and type 2 diabetes, what they can do to improve and reverse the disease progress, and most importantly how to implement these changes in the context of their own lives.
The program starts with a lifestyle medicine consultation where patients are provided a detailed action plan based on their own behaviour, biometric and lab data.
Patients work through a 12-week curriculum supported with weekly group medical visits. Each week teaches a core behaviour of the 4+2 Diabetes Reversal Strategy and progresses through three phases: foundations, nutrition and fine-tuning.
This is the evidence-based strategy that is the basis for the program. The scientific foundations of the program is the work by Dr. Roy Taylor, whose body of work, including the DiRECT study, demonstrated that type 2 diabetes can be reversed and that remission is possible for up to half of patients.
In practice, insulin resistance is reversible with significant weight loss of up to 15%. Improvement of pancreatic function is more variable and depends on a patient’s genetics and duration of diabetes. Data from our work shows that 92% of patients have significant insulin resistance that will benefit from the 4+2 strategy. The key elements of the strategy are:
Eat to lower Insulin - We teach the principles of whole-food, low glycemic load, adequate protein and fibre. These principles can be applied to any dietary or cultural preference.
Use your muscles - Recognizing the important role that muscles play in metabolic health, we encourage 150-300 minutes of exercise per week building gradually from easy aerobic activity.
Be kind to your liver - Since insulin resistance is driven by hepatic fat, we pay special attention to the role of alcohol, fructose and gut inflammation on the liver.
Restore fat burning - Ultimately the reversal of insulin resistance is dependent on losing hepatic fat. Nutritional changes, time-restricted eating and aerobic exercise all help to improve the ability to use stored fat for energy.
The +2 reflect the role of stress and sleep play on type 2 diabetes, specifically through the impact of cortisol.
