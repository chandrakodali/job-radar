Programme outline
Our programme, created by senior data analysts, is focused on helping you get hired and apply your skills from day one after graduation.
Core data analyst toolkit
Learn the skills and tools essential for data analytics. First, you’ll understand how to approach data analysis in a structured way, collect and interpret data, identify relevant patterns, and perform basic data visualisation. Then, you’ll master the use of spreadsheets and SQL, which are the core tools for this profession.
Communicating analysis results
Turn your data insights into compelling stories that inform and persuade your audience. You’ll be able to choose between Power BI and Tableau as a BI tool to create interactive dashboards that showcase your data analysis. You’ll also learn how to deliver engaging presentations highlighting your key findings, tailor your message to different audiences, use body language to enhance your communication, and select the best visualisations for your data.
Main analysis types
In this module, we’ll look at the main analysis types as they relate to four different fields: customer behaviour, e-commerce, customer value, and modelling. This will cover analysis types such as retention, cohorts, RFM, CLV, funnel, A/B testing, linear and logistic regression models, and more. These techniques will allow your analysis and insights to be robust and data-driven.
Python for data analytics
In this module, we'll take you on a journey through Python, even if you have no prior experience with Python or coding. Through hands-on projects and real-world examples, you'll gain the skills to tackle complex data challenges. From mastering the fundamentals of programming to working with libraries like Pandas, you'll learn how to process, analyse, and visualise data in ways that drive meaningful insights.
Optional modules
Tailor your learning journey to align with your specific goals and interests. Enhance the core curriculum by exploring specialisations such as product or marketing analytics. Boost your job search prospects by completing an optional capstone project. Here, you’ll create a distinctive portfolio piece that showcases your acquired knowledge.

FAQ
Do I need coding experience to study data analytics?
You don’t need previous Python experience. The programme begins with spreadsheets and SQL, then introduces Python fundamentals and how to use it for analysis.
You’ll develop your coding skills through projects, connecting what you learn to questions about customers, products and business performance.
How much maths will I use?
You’ll work with percentages, averages, comparisons and statistics to understand patterns and evaluate findings. The programme introduces core statistics before progressing to statistical inference and A/B testing.
You’ll apply these concepts to questions, such as whether customer behaviour differs between groups or whether an experiment supports a change. If your maths feels rusty, discuss preparation with admissions.
Can I study alongside a full-time job?
Yes. The programme offers flexible pacing over six to 10 months, with at least 15 hours of focused study each week.
Set aside regular time for learning, projects and reviews. Experience with customers, budgets, operations or performance reports can give you useful context for the questions you’ll investigate.
Should I choose data analytics, data science or AI engineering?
Choose based on the work you want to do:
Data Analytics : investigate business questions, build dashboards and communicate findings that help teams make decisions.
Data Science & AI : go deeper into statistics and machine learning to build and evaluate predictive models.
AI Engineering : build applications using large language models and AI agents, building on existing coding experience.
These capabilities can support different career directions. Compare the curricula and entry requirements with your background and goals.
What kinds of business questions will I learn to answer?
You’ll investigate why customers leave, where people drop out before purchasing and how customer groups behave differently. You’ll also explore how to compare campaign performance and assess A/B test results.
These methods help you explain your findings and recommend what a team should investigate or change next.
Will I build a portfolio employers can review?
Yes. You’ll complete projects that demonstrate how you analyse data, visualise findings and make recommendations. An optional independent capstone lets you develop a portfolio piece around your industry interests.
Use your work to show employers how you approach a question, choose methods and explain your conclusions.
Explore learner work in the Turing College project showcase .
What is the difference between product analytics and marketing analytics?
Product analytics investigates how people use a product: where they encounter problems, which features they use and what keeps them returning.
Marketing analytics investigates campaign performance, customer behaviour and the steps people take towards a purchase.
Both use data to guide decisions. Choose the area that connects with your interests, existing experience and career goals.
Is data analytics still worth learning as AI tools improve?
Yes. AI tools can help write queries, create charts and draft explanations. Analytical skills help you check whether those outputs are accurate and useful.
You’ll learn to define a question, spot problems in data and judge whether a conclusion is justified. Your knowledge of a business or industry helps you connect the findings to decisions that matter.
Will I learn about AI and machine learning?
Yes. The introductory module explores AI applications, and the Python module introduces regression and classification through predictive modelling.
For greater depth in machine learning, compare the Data Science & AI programme . If your goal is building LLM applications and AI agents using existing coding skills, explore AI Engineering .
What career opportunities can these skills support?
You can work towards roles in data analytics, reporting, business intelligence, marketing analytics or product analytics. You can also apply the skills in operations, finance, marketing and other fields where interpreting data is part of the work.
Your previous experience can help you choose a direction. Understanding an industry’s customers, processes or priorities gives you context for asking useful questions and explaining the findings.
Can I move into analytical work without a relevant degree?
Yes. A relevant degree is one route, while other routes combine analytical skills, project work and experience from another field.
Your portfolio gives employers evidence of how you investigate questions and communicate results. Some positions require specific qualifications, so focus your applications on opportunities that match your skills and background.
How does Turing College support my next career move?
A self-paced career module covers job-search strategy, your CV and LinkedIn profile. You’ll also have HR and technical mock interviews, alongside resources in the Career Guidance Hub and access to career support chat.
Use this support to prepare applications and practise explaining how your experience and project work fit the opportunities you want to pursue. Employment is not guaranteed.
