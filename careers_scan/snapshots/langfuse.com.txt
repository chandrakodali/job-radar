Why join Langfuse

You own an area, and your work is public
Engineers own a feature area and stay with it as the team grows. Marketers own launches, events, or a program like Langfuse Academy. The people who build a feature answer users in GitHub Discussions themselves. Because the work is open source, your track record stays public.
You own it from the customer call to the launch. Here is how that looked for one real feature, the evals revamp:
Customer. It starts with users telling us in GitHub Discussions that mapping data into evaluators was painful. ( How we work with customers )
RFC. You write the RFC. The team reviews it async, then we settle the hard questions in one intense session. ( Roadmapping )
Build. Then you build. A lot. The main PR touched 600 files. ( How we ship )
Ship. You launch it with the GTM team: changelog , docs and marketing. ( Releases )
Offsite. Then you meet everyone in person at an offsite in Tokyo or Berlin. ( Remote at Langfuse )
ClickHouse backs us and stays out of the way
We set our own roadmap. ClickHouse gives us a global sales team that sells Langfuse and a much larger audience for our launches. The engineers who build the database are one message away.
Our bet is that ClickHouse will be one of the great infrastructure companies of the AI era, and that joining Langfuse now means being part of that. The database already runs real-time analytics, observability, and AI workloads. Langfuse is how that platform sees what agents do in production. For more background, see below .
AI-pilled and remote
Everyone gets a very high AI budget. We build slides with AI, maintain our own coding harness, and almost everyone runs a small army of agents. We have three scheduled meetings a week. Everything else happens in writing (see our principles ).
